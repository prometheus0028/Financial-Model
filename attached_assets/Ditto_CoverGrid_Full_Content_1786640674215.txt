PHASE 1 — STRATEGIC EVALUATION
====================================================================

TITLE
Strategic Evaluation: The Ditto–CoverGrid Merger
Subtitle: Unlocking Workplace Distribution While Protecting the Trust-First Advisory Model
Presenter: M&A Strategy Team

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- The Verdict: Proceed with a Renegotiated Merger.
- Financial Stance: Accept the proposed financial terms of a 30% equity stake for CoverGrid and a ₹25 Crore cash consideration. These terms are highly accretive for Ditto given CoverGrid's net debt position.
- Strategic Rationale: The merger secures direct access to 640 employers and nearly 5.8 lakh employees, effectively solving Ditto's customer acquisition bottleneck.
- The Critical Condition: Ditto must firmly reject the proposal allowing CoverGrid's founders to run the enterprise division autonomously for three years. Autonomy threatens Ditto's trust-first brand and introduces severe mis-selling risks.

STRATEGIC RATIONALE & THE INSURANCE-EDUCATION GAP
- Market Shift: Employer-sponsored insurance is rapidly becoming a primary distribution channel in India.
- The Gap: The workplace is where employees first encounter health insurance, but they often lack an understanding of their coverage limitations.
- The Synergy: CoverGrid currently owns the employer relationships and enrollment operations. Ditto owns the B2C trust and advisory expertise.
- Value Creation: By fusing these capabilities, Ditto will insert its advisory layer directly into the workplace, cross-selling personal health and term insurance to a captive audience with near-zero acquisition cost.

TRANSACTION ARCHETYPE & MARKET COMPARABLES
- Archetype: This is a Complementary Trust-Brand + Distribution-Channel deal, not a standard cost-synergy roll-up.
- Tier A Precedent (Success): Marsh & McLennan's acquisition of JLT. Similarity Score: 74/100.
  Lesson: A successful advisory-and-distribution combination requires absorbing the acquired entity's leadership into unified executive roles rather than leaving them in walled-off divisions.
- Tier C Precedent (Failure): Aon–WTW proposed merger. Similarity Score: 38/100.
  Lesson: Pre-emptive integration and divestiture commitments carry massive financial penalties if a deal collapses. Integration spending must be strictly sequenced.

VALUATION ASSESSMENT: ARE THE PROPOSED TERMS FAIR?
The non-binding proposal asks for a 30% equity stake for CoverGrid + ₹25 Cr cash consideration.

Pre-Adjustment Baseline:
- Revenue Contribution: CoverGrid accounts for 53.8% (174/323) of combined FY26 revenue.
- EBITDA Contribution: CoverGrid accounts for 59.2% (16/27) of combined FY26 EBITDA.
- At first glance, receiving only 30% equity when contributing >50% of the financial metrics makes the deal look heavily skewed in Ditto's favor.

Adjustments for Debt and Quality (why 30% makes sense):
- Net Debt Penalty: CoverGrid has ₹28 Cr in debt and only ₹14 Cr in cash (Net Debt of ₹14 Cr). Ditto is entirely debt-free with ₹42 Cr in cash. Enterprise-to-equity value bridges heavily penalize CoverGrid.
- Quality of Earnings (NPS & Churn): CoverGrid's NPS has declined to 42, and employee satisfaction is a poor 68%. Ditto boasts an NPS of 74 and 91% employee satisfaction. CoverGrid is buying into Ditto's premium brand equity to save its deteriorating customer experience.
- The ₹25 Cr Cash Outflow: The cash consideration effectively drains Ditto's balance sheet to pay out CoverGrid investors today, lowering the equity stake they deserve in the combined future entity.

Phase 2 Conclusion: The proposed terms (30% + ₹25 Cr) are highly favorable to Ditto and represent a fair, risk-adjusted price. While CoverGrid brings higher Day-1 scale, Ditto is the "value engine" of this transaction — the brand, the high NPS, the net-cash balance sheet, and the advisory capability that will unlock the synergies. Accepting this structure means acquiring a massive distribution channel at a significant discount to raw revenue footprint.

PHASE 3 — DEAL STRUCTURE, INTEGRATION & GOVERNANCE
====================================================================

ALTERNATIVE DEAL STRUCTURES & DECISION MATRIX
Ditto's Board evaluated five transaction pathways, scored against the Board's explicit constraints: preserving the trust model, maximizing long-term value, and minimizing integration risk.

Option | Value Creation | Trust/Brand Risk | Integration Risk | Verdict
1. Proceed on Proposed Terms | High (captures 5.8L base) | Severe | High | Reject. Allowing founders to run the enterprise arm autonomously for 3 years protects their commission-driven culture, risking Ditto's brand.
2. Renegotiate (Recommended) | High | Moderate (Controlled) | Moderate | Accept. Accept the financial terms (30% + ₹25 Cr) but strictly renegotiate the governance and founder autonomy clauses.
3. Acquire Minority Stake | Low | Low | Low | Reject. Does not grant Ditto the authority to fix CoverGrid's declining 42 NPS.
4. Strategic Partnership | Medium | Low | Low | Backup. A simple referral agreement avoids capital outlay but yields terrible conversion rates compared to native platform integration.
5. Walk Away | Zero | Zero | Zero | Reject. The opportunity to capture employees at the start of their financial journey is too strategically valuable.

The Strategy: The financial terms (30% equity + ₹25 Cr cash) are accretive given CoverGrid's net debt, but the governance terms are toxic. Ditto must renegotiate the founders' 3-year autonomous leadership clause into a standard 12-month earn-out with strict adherence to Ditto's compliance metrics.

INTEGRATION ROADMAP & MIS-SELLING GOVERNANCE
The operational gap is significant: Ditto thrives on unbiased advice (91% ESAT, 74 NPS) while CoverGrid relies on aggressive selling and commissions (68% ESAT, 42 NPS). Integrating these models requires a phased, heavy-handed approach to governance.

- Day 0 to 30 (Pre-Close & Month 1) — Data Harmonization & Security: Drawing a direct lesson from the UnitedHealth–Change Healthcare disaster, integrating IT systems without harmonizing security controls is an existential risk. A full audit of CoverGrid's data on its 5.8 lakh employees must be completed before network environments are bridged.
- Month 1 to 3 — Compensation Redesign: Mis-Selling Governance: to stop aggressive selling, CoverGrid's sales incentives must be dismantled. Reps transition from high-variable commission structures to Ditto's model — higher base salaries with bonuses tied strictly to Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) and policy retention, not just volume.
- Month 6 to 12 — Technology Unification: Sunsetting CoverGrid's legacy claims management and enrollment portal. All 640 employer clients migrate to a unified "Ditto Workplace" backend, enabling seamless cross-selling of retail insurance within a single user interface.
- Month 18 — Brand Phase-Out: As stipulated in the proposal, the CoverGrid brand exists for 18 months. During this window it co-brands as "CoverGrid by Ditto" before being fully retired and absorbed into the Ditto master brand.

BRAND & CULTURE STRATEGY
The most significant non-financial threat to this deal is cultural contagion. CoverGrid's low employee satisfaction (68%) indicates burnout, likely stemming from its aggressive sales culture.
- Quarantine, then Assimilate: CoverGrid teams initially operate in a separate division to prevent their cultural norms from bleeding into Ditto's advisory teams.
- Leadership Implants: Rather than letting CoverGrid founders run the division entirely, Ditto places its own seasoned culture leaders into CoverGrid's HR and mid-level management immediately upon closing.
- Upskilling to Advisory: CoverGrid agents go through Ditto's rigorous insurance-education training program. Those who cannot transition from "salesmen" to "advisors" are managed out.

THE WALK-AWAY PRICE (BOARD RED LINES)
While the proposed financial terms are acceptable, M&A negotiations are fluid. Ditto's Board must define hard red lines. Ditto should walk away if CoverGrid demands:
- Cash consideration exceeding ₹35 Crore: Ditto currently has ₹42 Crore in cash. Paying more than ₹35 Cr would severely deplete working capital, limiting Ditto's ability to fund the integration and absorb CoverGrid's ₹28 Crore debt load.
- Equity stake exceeding 38%: Handing over nearly 40% of the combined entity over-values CoverGrid's low-quality revenue (declining NPS and high debt) and under-values Ditto's premium brand equity.
- Refusal to yield on Founder Control: If CoverGrid's founders refuse to subject their operations to Ditto's strict mis-selling audits and CSAT-based incentive structures, the deal is un-investable. Trust is Ditto's only moat; a rogue enterprise division will destroy it.

NEXT STEPS & IMMEDIATE ACTIONS
1. Formally reject the "3-year autonomous leadership" clause in the current non-binding proposal.
2. Issue a revised term sheet holding the 30% equity and ₹25 Crore cash lines, but introducing the 12-month CSAT-linked earn-out.
3. Initiate Phase 1 Due Diligence, heavily indexing on CoverGrid's IT security architecture and historic mis-selling/claims dispute data.

2. What the Simulator Does
- Five sliders let the user adjust: CoverGrid Equity Stake (10–50%), Cash Consideration (₹0–50 Cr), Ditto Revenue Growth Yr5 Taper (10–50%), CoverGrid Revenue Growth Yr5 Taper (5–45%), and Yr5 Revenue Synergy (₹0–150 Cr).
- A live stacked bar + line chart shows Ditto Revenue, CoverGrid Revenue, and Synergy Revenue stacked by year (FY26–FY31), with Combined EBITDA plotted as an overlaid line on a secondary axis.
- Growth rates taper linearly each year from a starting point (45% for Ditto, 44% for CoverGrid) down to the user-selected Year 5 target.
- EBITDA margins interpolate linearly from the FY26 base margin to the Year 5 target margin.
- Synergy revenue scales up linearly from ₹0 in Year 0 to the user-selected Year 5 synergy value, and is assumed to convert at a high 40% EBITDA margin (since it's cross-sell on an existing base with near-zero CAC).
- A live "Deal Assessment" box flips between Accretive (green) and Value Destructive (red) based on the same Board red-line thresholds used in Phase 3: equity stake above 38% or cash consideration above ₹35 Cr triggers the "dilutive" verdict; otherwise the deal is shown as accretive.

        function calculateProjections() {

            const cash = parseInt(document.getElementById('input-cash').value);
            const dittoGrowthY5 = parseInt(document.getElementById('input-ditto-growth').value) / 100;

            let cgRev = [CG_REV_BASE];
